    Cover, as named sections:

    1. **Quem fala e para quem** — the brand's voice as a restaurant at the Mercado da Torre in
       Recife, addressing a mobile-first local audience looking for something informal to eat.
       State the register explicitly: jovem, descontraído, urbano. State the person and pronoun
       choice (second person, `você`) and commit to it consistently.
    2. **O conceito central** — that every piece of copy ultimately serves one idea: "Mais que um
       pão de alho!". The garlic bread is the signature, not the whole menu, and copy must never
       read like a generic hamburgueria.
    3. **Ritmo e forma** — short sentences, active voice, concrete nouns over adjectives, headings
       that fit a phone screen without wrapping three times. Guidance on when a promotional
       handwritten/script phrase is appropriate (rare, promotional lines only) and when it is not.
    4. **Regionalismo** — how far to lean into Recife/Pernambuco vernacular: enough to sound local
       and warm, not so far that it excludes a visitor who is not from there.
    5. **O que nunca fazer** — the hard list. Never invent a product, a price, a rating, an award,
       an operating hour, or a commercial promise. Never use superlatives or comparisons about the
       business that no source supports. Never write a claim that would need a lawyer or a
       nutritionist to defend. Never let informality slide into mocking, body-shaming, or
       guilt-based urgency ("você vai se arrepender se não pedir agora").
    6. **Acessibilidade da linguagem** — link text must describe its destination on its own
       ("Pedir no iFood", not "clique aqui"); avoid relying on emoji to carry meaning; alt text
       describes the food, not the file.
    7. **Exemplos aprovados e reprovados** — at least six paired examples, each showing a rejected
       line and its approved rewrite with a one-line reason. Draw the approved lines only from
       facts PROJECT.md confirms (the concept, the location, the modalities, the iFood channel).
       Where an example needs a product or price, use an explicit placeholder token and say so.

    Close with a short note stating what this file is not: it is not the final copy for any
    section. Per D-04, final wording for hero and location is written in Phase 2 and for the menu
    in Phase 3; this file is the constraint those phases write inside.

<threat_model>
## Trust Boundaries

| Boundary | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| authored content → published site | Copy written here is read by visitors as authoritative statements by a real business. The author is an agent, not the business owner. |
| research findings → asserted fact | Information gathered from Instagram/iFood during discovery is stale or unverified; treating it as confirmed publishes a claim nobody stands behind. |

## STRIDE Threat Register

| Threat ID | Category | Component | Severity | Disposition | Mitigation Plan |
|-----------|----------|-----------|----------|-------------|-----------------|
| T-01-12 | Spoofing | authored copy asserting unconfirmed facts | high | mitigate | `confirmed` / `pendingConfirmation` is a required field on every skeleton entry, enforced by a test; the tone guide's "nunca fazer" list names each forbidden invention class; no price, product, or opening time may appear unless PROJECT.md confirms it (CONT-03, D-05). |
| T-01-13 | Tampering | a later phase treating the skeleton as final copy | medium | mitigate | Every entry carries `writtenInPhase`, and the tone guide closes with an explicit statement of which phase writes each section's final wording (D-04). |
| T-01-14 | Information Disclosure | contact/location content | low | accept | All information in scope is already public (a street address and a public Instagram profile); no personal data of staff or customers is introduced. |
| T-01-01 | Tampering / Spoofing | CTA destination labels in the skeleton | medium | mitigate | The skeleton names CTA *labels and intent only*; every actual href is built by plan 01-02's allowlisted integration module, so no URL literal enters the content layer (INTEGRA-04, SEC-03). |
</threat_model>
